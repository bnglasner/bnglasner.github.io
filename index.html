Ben Glasner

This is Ben

I’m Ben Glasner, a Senior Economist at the Economic Innovation Group. I work on questions at the intersection of labor markets, place, and social-policy design—especially where the economy fails to turn work into stability and upward mobility.

Featured work

  • How to end low-wage work forever — a wage-subsidy proposal aimed at raising take-home pay without discouraging employment.
  • The Great Transfer-mation — analysis of how many U.S. communities became more reliant on government-transfer income.
  • Opportunity Zones & housing supply — work on place-based policy and housing stock outcomes.

What I work on

  • Wages, work, and labor-market institutions (including wage-subsidy design)
  • Economic geography and political economy (place-based outcomes; geographic divides)
  • Transfers, safety net programs, and poverty dynamics (including intergenerational mobility and food assistance)
  • Opportunity Zones and housing supply (place-based policy evaluation, housing stock outcomes)
  • Gig economy and the future of work (my dissertation-era focus; policy implications)

Background

I’m currently a Senior Economist at the Economic Innovation Group (EIG). Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Research Scientist at Columbia University’s Center on Poverty and Social Policy, where I worked on policies affecting poverty and well-being, including the Child Tax Credit.

I earned my Ph.D. in Public Policy and Management from the University of Washington’s Daniel J. Evans School of Public Policy and Governance.
