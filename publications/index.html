Work, wages, and labor-market institutions

Minimum wages, gig and nonstandard work, noncompete agreements, remote work, wage-subsidy design, and how the typical American worker is faring.

  1. PEER-REVIEWED · 2023

    The Minimum Wage, Self-Employment, and the Online Gig Economy

    Minimum-wage increases pushed some workers into gig and nonemployer self-employment, concentrated in transportation and warehousing after ride-hailing platforms emerged.

    Benjamin Glasner
    Journal of Labor Economics DOI Details
  2. POLICY REPORT · 2025

    How to end low-wage work forever: An 80-80 wage subsidy proposal

    The 80-80 proposal would subsidize low-wage paychecks directly, raising take-home pay without adding to employers’ cost of hiring.

    Benjamin Glasner, Adam Ozimek
    Economic Innovation Group Website Details
  3. POLICY REPORT · 2024

    The American Worker Project: Toward a New Consensus

    American workers are more prosperous than prior generations by most measures, but the pace of improvement has slowed, and public perception often lags the underlying data.

    Adam Ozimek, John Lettieri, Benjamin Glasner
    Economic Innovation GroupPDF Website Details
  4. POLICY REPORT · 2023

    Full vs. Hybrid: Examining the Consequences of How Americans Work Remotely

    Full-remote work raises long-distance migration and destination-market housing prices, while hybrid work enables shorter moves without the same price effect.

    Eric Carlson, Benjamin Glasner, Adam Ozimek
    Economic Innovation Group Website Details
  5. POLICY REPORT · 2023

    The Effects of Noncompete Agreement Reforms on Business Formation: A Comparison of Hawaii and Oregon

    Hawaii’s noncompete exemption for technology workers was associated with 10 percent more technology establishments, while Oregon’s broader low-wage-worker reform showed no significant effect.

    Benjamin Glasner
    Economic Innovation GroupPDF Website Details
  6. DISSERTATION · 2021

    The Impact of Public Policy on Nonstandard Work Arrangements

    Doctoral dissertation on how public policy shapes nonstandard work arrangements, completed at the University of Washington.

    Benjamin Glasner
    University of Washington Details

The safety net, poverty, and mobility

How the Child Tax Credit, the Earned Income Tax Credit, food stamps, liquid savings, and government transfers shape work, well-being, hardship, and the intergenerational persistence of poverty.

  1. PEER-REVIEWED · 2024

    Effects of the expanded Child Tax Credit on employment outcomes

    The expanded Child Tax Credit’s monthly payments reduced poverty and hardship without reducing parental employment or labor force participation.

    Elizabeth Ananat, Benjamin Glasner, Christal Hamilton, Zachary Parolin, Clemente Pignatti
    Journal of Public Economics DOI Details
  2. PEER-REVIEWED · 2022

    No Evidence the Child Tax Credit Expansion Had an Effect on the Well-Being and Mental Health of Parents

    The expanded Child Tax Credit showed no measurable short-term effect on parents’ life satisfaction, anxiety, or depression symptoms.

    Benjamin Glasner, Oscar Jiménez-Solomon, Sophie Collyer, Irwin Garfinkel, Christopher Wimer
    Health Affairs DOI Details
  3. WORKING PAPER · 2025

    The Earned Income Tax Credit and the Intergenerational Persistence of Poverty

    The EITC improves children’s long-run pre-tax economic circumstances but does not reduce the relative intergenerational persistence of poverty or any persistence measure based on post-tax income.

    Zachary Parolin, Benjamin Glasner, Ronald Mincy, Christopher Wimer
    Institute for New Economic Thinking at the Oxford Martin School, University of OxfordPDF Website Details
  4. WORKING PAPER · 2023

    The Effectiveness of the Food Stamp Program at Reducing Racial Differences in the Intergenerational Persistence of Poverty

    Childhood access to the Food Stamp Program reduced adult poverty by five percentage points overall, with a nine-point reduction in deep poverty for Black adults whose parents lacked a high school education.

    Benjamin Glasner, Ronald B. Mincy, Zachary Parolin, Christopher Wimer
    Washington Center for Equitable GrowthPDF Website Details
  5. POLICY REPORT · 2024

    The Great "Transfer"-mation: How American Communities Became Reliant on Income from Government

    Government transfers grew from 8 percent of personal income in 1970 to almost 18 percent by 2022, leaving roughly half of U.S. counties substantially reliant on transfer income.

    Kenan Fikri, Sarah Eckhardt, Benjamin Glasner
    Economic Innovation GroupPDF Website Code Details
  6. POLICY REPORT · 2023

    Liquid Assets, Financial Shocks, and Entrances into Material Hardship

    New Yorkers with $2,000 in liquid assets are substantially less likely to fall into material hardship after a financial shock than those with none, and the protective effect levels off above that amount.

    Matthew Maury, Sophie Collyer, Benjamin Glasner, Yajun Jia, Christopher Wimer, Christopher Yera
    Center on Poverty and Social Policy at Columbia University (Robin Hood Poverty Tracker Spotlight)PDF Website Details

Place-based policy and housing

Opportunity Zones and the geography of investment and housing supply.

  1. POLICY REPORT · 2025

    The Impact of Opportunity Zones on Housing Supply

    Opportunity Zone designation increased new housing construction by 70 percent in designated tracts, adding more than 416,000 residential addresses with little displacement from nearby areas.

    Benjamin Glasner, Adam Ozimek, John Lettieri
    Economic Innovation GroupPDF Website Code Details
  2. POLICY REPORT · 2023

    Are Opportunity Zones Working? What the Literature Tells Us

    Studies that wait long enough to observe Opportunity Zones under fully finalized regulations find the strongest positive investment effects; many widely cited early studies end their analysis window too soon to detect them.

    Kenan Fikri, Benjamin Glasner
    Economic Innovation GroupPDF Website Details
