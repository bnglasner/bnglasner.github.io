research

Research on labor markets, place-based policy, and social-policy design.

Wages, Work & Labor-Market Institutions

How labor-market rules and wage policy shape the well-being of workers—especially those at the bottom of the earnings distribution. This line of work ranges from a comprehensive wage-subsidy proposal to empirical studies of minimum-wage spillovers into the gig economy and the effects of noncompete reform on business formation.

  • How to end low-wage work forever: An 80-80 wage subsidy proposal (EIG, 2025) — A wage-subsidy proposal aimed at raising take-home pay without discouraging employment.
  • The American Worker: Toward a New Consensus (EIG, 2024) — Mapping the state of the American worker and building a case for new labor-market policy.
  • The Minimum Wage, Self-Employment, and the Online Gig Economy (Journal of Labor Economics, 2023) — Evidence that minimum-wage increases push workers toward self-employment and online gig platforms.
  • The Effects of Noncompete Agreement Reforms on Business Formation (EIG, 2023) — Comparing Hawaii and Oregon reforms to understand how noncompete bans affect entrepreneurship.

Place-Based Policy & Economic Geography

Evaluating place-based tools—especially Opportunity Zones—and documenting the geographic transformation of income sources across American communities.

  • The Impact of Opportunity Zones on Housing Supply (EIG, 2025) — New evidence on how OZ designation affects housing stock. [Code]
  • The Great “Transfer”-mation (EIG, 2024) — Analysis of how many U.S. communities became more reliant on government-transfer income. [Code]
  • Are Opportunity Zones Working? What the Literature Tells Us (EIG, 2023) — A survey of the empirical literature on OZ outcomes.

Transfers, Safety Net & Poverty

Research on how safety-net programs—particularly the Child Tax Credit and food assistance—affect employment, health, and intergenerational poverty.

  • Effects of the expanded Child Tax Credit on employment outcomes (Journal of Public Economics, 2024) — Examining whether the 2021 CTC expansion reduced parental employment.
  • No Evidence the Child Tax Credit Expansion Had an Effect on the Well-Being and Mental Health of Parents (Health Affairs, 2022) — Testing for mental-health effects of the expanded CTC.
  • The Effectiveness of the Food Stamp Program at Reducing Racial Differences in the Intergenerational Persistence of Poverty (Equitable Growth, 2023) — Whether early-life food assistance breaks cycles of poverty.
  • Liquid Assets, Financial Shocks, and Entrances into Material Hardship (Robin Hood, 2023) — How liquid-asset buffers protect families from financial shocks.

Gig Economy & the Future of Work

My dissertation-era focus: understanding how public policy shapes nonstandard work arrangements, from ride-hail driving to freelance platforms.

  • The Impact of Public Policy on Nonstandard Work Arrangements (Dissertation, University of Washington, 2021)
